The pursuit

I wrote AI for Gen Z without knowing how to write a book. I did not know how to market one either, or how to get it published. I had an idea and a willingness to execute. Sometimes that is all you need.

Once you have resources and experience, making the process you struggled through easier for the next person becomes the obvious thing to do. That is what Mantic is for.

We give each idea careful attention and real time. Young authors especially tend to arrive with a beautiful nugget and think the nugget is the book. I know it is not. The early versions of AI for Gen Z, and of The Transcendence of Technology, were a far cry from what they became. The only way I found the wider idea was through deliberate, painful, necessary conversation. People around a table, physical or digital, as interested in your idea as you are. That is the fuel.

At every point you have access to marketing, creative and spiritual resources. There is no expectation that you use all of them. Think of a grocery store. Nobody buys the whole shelf. You come in knowing roughly what you need, you take it, you leave. Mantic is the store. The author chooses.

Once the author and the publisher are both happy with the book, it goes to marketing. We work with book marketers on packaging and distribution, and with social media people who build a three month and six month sprint so an audience is waiting when the book lands.

Last, and most important: we do not publish books we do not believe in. Our model is not built on signing a quota or chasing big names. Those two habits ruin publishers, and a ruined publisher damages more than its own list.

In a world of mega publishers and self publishing, Mantic sits in a category of its own. Not defined by the mainstream narrative, but by spiritual innerstanding.

Ayush Prakash
